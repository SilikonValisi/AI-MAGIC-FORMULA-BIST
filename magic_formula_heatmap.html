Financing / EBIT is now a true trailing-twelve-month figure on both sides (fixed an earlier version that compared a YTD financing-expense number against a full-year EBIT). Rows with negative EBIT show EBIT neg. instead of a ratio, since dividing by a loss doesn't describe leverage risk the same way.

Debt / Capital is a second, different leverage lens: it's the share of each company's capital employed (the same base RoC is computed against) that's funded by net debt rather than equity. A high value here means a good RoC rank is substantially leverage-driven, not pure capital efficiency — negative values mean the company is sitting on net cash. Unlike Financing / EBIT, this one stays meaningful even when EBIT is currently negative. The dot on Volume flags average daily trading under 5 mn$.
